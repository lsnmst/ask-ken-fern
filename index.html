You're probably arguing a little annoyed, aren't you? "Non-colonised", in Europe?

Non-colonised edible plants are plants that people can eat but that have never become, or are no longer part of, the dominant food system. They may have been overlooked, pushed aside or simply forgotten as food, even though people in particular places have continued to know, gather, grow and eat them. What makes a plant “non-colonised” is therefore not whether it is rare, wild or native, but the history of how it has been treated and valued as food.

In Europe, this can include native plants, plants that have become naturalised, as well as plants introduced in the past. What they have in common is that their edible uses have survived in particular communities and places through rural and local traditions, Indigenous and migrant knowledge, or other forms of everyday food practice while remaining largely outside mainstream food culture, agriculture and markets.

Read more about the concept of "non-colonised edible plants" in the work of Antônio Bispo dos Santos.
